# Portfolio defence speech (about 5 minutes)

Good morning, dear teachers and classmates.

My name is Khaldar Aigerim. I am a fourth-year student of English Language Education at Akhmet Baitursynuly Regional University. Today I want to show you my e-portfolio. I made it as a website, so you can open it on your phone or on a computer. It has ten tasks. Every task has its own page and its own colour.

On the first page you can see a short text about me and the contents with all ten tasks. You just tap a title, and the task opens.

Task one is an interview with a school English teacher. The topic was group work. I asked her five questions, and I show our talk as a Telegram chat. The main idea from her answers is simple: group work helps students speak more, but it needs clear roles, a time limit and feedback at the end.

Task two is rules for group work in the eleventh grade. I wrote ten rules and put them in three steps: before we start, while we work, and at the end. Under every rule there is a short example from a real lesson. I also added group roles: leader, timekeeper, speaker and secretary. And there are some useful phrases, for example, "Could you say more about that?"

Task three is a quest on critical thinking for the seventh grade. The topic is travelling. There are five tasks, from easy to difficult. First, students guess famous places from clues. Then they watch a short video from the British Council. Then they decide if a sentence is a fact or an opinion. After that, they solve a logic puzzle, and at the end they write a short opinion text. Every online task has a link, and the text on my page is the same as on the website.

Task four is two rubrics. The first one is analytic, for an essay. It checks content, structure, language and argument one by one. The second one is holistic, for a monologue. Here the teacher gives one score for the whole answer. I understood that an analytic rubric is better when students need detailed feedback, and a holistic rubric is faster.

Task five is reading tasks for gifted and talented students in the sixth grade. I took a real text from the textbook English Plus 6 on okulyk.kz, page 34. The text is about animals in danger. The book asks simple questions. My tasks ask students to analyse the text, to find facts and opinions, to give their own opinion, and to make a fact file about an animal from Kazakhstan's red list.

Task six is an essay about the reasons for underachievement. In my opinion, pupils usually don't fail because they are not clever. The reasons are often problems at home, low motivation, boring lessons or stress.

Task seven is a memo on Lesson Study. It is a short letter to teachers. Lesson Study came from Japan. Teachers plan a lesson together, one teacher teaches it, the others watch how the students learn, and then they discuss it and make the lesson better.

Task eight is two leadership tests. The first test is for students, and the second one is for teachers. You can try both tests right on the website and see your result.

Task nine is my questionnaire about inclusive education. I made it in Google Forms. It has ten questions, and every question has five answers.

And task ten shows the results. Fourteen people answered. Ten of them said that inclusive education is absolutely essential. Half of them said that the biggest problem is weak training for teachers. So my main conclusion is this: a good attitude is not enough. Teachers need real, practical skills.

To finish, this portfolio helped me see what a teacher really does every day: plan, check, ask questions and listen to students. I also learned to check every source and every number.

Thank you for listening. I am happy to answer your questions.
